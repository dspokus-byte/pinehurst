How the Handicap System Works
A fair, self-correcting competition system for golfers of all skill levels
The Goal

This system keeps the competition fair and close, even when players have different skill levels or haven't updated their official handicap recently. Each player starts with an agreed handicap and earns adjustments daily based on how they actually play.

Starting Handicaps & Trip Limits

Before the trip begins, each player is assigned a starting handicap agreed on by the group. It should reflect how the player is actually playing today — not an outdated number.

From that starting handicap, two limits are set for the entire trip and never change:

Floor
Starting handicap minus 2
The lowest your handicap can go
Ceiling
Starting handicap plus 2
The highest your handicap can go
Example: Player 1 starts at 17. Their floor is 15 and ceiling is 19 for the whole trip — no matter what happens.
Course Handicap (Auto-Calculated)

Each course plays differently. Before each round, your handicap index is automatically converted to a course handicap using the USGA formula. This happens in the app as soon as a course and tee are selected in Configuration.

Course Handicap = Index × (Slope ÷ 113) + (Rating − Par)
Example: Player 1 has an index of 17. The course has slope 130, rating 69.6, par 72.
17 × (130 ÷ 113) + (69.6 − 72) = 19.54 − 2.4 = 17 course handicap
Daily Adjustment Rules

After each round, your net score is compared to par. The difference determines how your handicap adjusts for the next day. The maximum change in any single round is ±2 strokes.

Net vs Par Result Adjustment
−3 or better Strong overperformance −2 strokes
−1 to −2 Slight overperformance −1 stroke
Even to +2 Normal performance No change
+3 to +5 Underperformance +1 stroke
+6 or worse Major struggle +2 strokes

Player 1 started at 17, hit their floor of 15 after a great Day 2, then recovered to finish at 17. The floor prevented the handicap from dropping below 15 even with a −5 net.
Why This System?

Official handicaps don't always reflect how someone is playing today — especially if they haven't posted scores recently. This system self-corrects. If a player's starting handicap is too high, strong play will reduce it quickly. If it's too low, poor play will bump it back up.

The floor and ceiling prevent wild swings in either direction, keeping the competition tight across all days of the trip.
